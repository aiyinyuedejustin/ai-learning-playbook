## 正文

Students who used AI to study remembered less than those who did not.

It’s called “cognitive crutch”

Researchers split 120 students into two groups.

One group used ChatGPT as a learning aid to summarize, structure, and explain information. The other group had to do the hard, traditional work of researching and organizing the material manually.

During the study phase, the AI group looked unstoppable. They flew through answers. They finished early. They felt completely prepared.

Then came the surprise retention test 45 days later.

The traditional group crushed it with a 68.5% average.

The AI group plummeted to 57.5%.

An 11-point collapse in long-term memory.

Here is why it happens:

Your brain doesn’t store memories like a hard drive. Information doesn’t just "save" itself because you looked at it.

Durable memory requires cognitive friction. It requires the struggle of retrieval. It requires hitting a wall, feeling confused, and forcing your neural pathways to build the connection yourself.

When you use AI to study, it acts as a cognitive crutch.

It gives you the answer instantly. It removes the friction. It does the heavy lifting so your brain doesn't have to.

You experience the illusion of competence. Because the AI makes the material easy to understand in the moment, your brain assumes it has mastered it.

It hasn't.

You didn't learn the material. You just rented the AI's understanding for ten seconds.

The moment the tool is taken away, the knowledge vanishes.

We are trading long-term retention for short-term convenience. We are outsourcing our memory systems to software, and our brains are quietly atrophy-ing as a result.

AI is an incredible tool for acceleration. But if you use it to bypass the struggle of thinking, you aren't getting smarter.

You're just paying for your own forgetfulness.
