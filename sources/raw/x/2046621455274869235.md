The construction on these seem quite strong!

Looking through the first set, only a couple construction problems I see:

Q. What is the necessary and sufficient condition for a finite-dimensional linear operator $T$ to be **diagonalizable**?
A. The vector space $V$ must admit a basis consisting entirely of eigenvectors of $T$.

I've noticed that "the" in a question like this is usually a tell for ambiguity. e.g. an equally valid answer is "The minimal polynomial $m_T(x)$ factors as a product of pairwise distinct linear factors over the base field."

Q. Why is an algebraically closed field (like $\mathbb{C}$) the optimal setting for ensuring an operator has eigenvalues?
A. Because over $\mathbb{C}$, the characteristic polynomial always splits completely into linear factors, guaranteeing that roots (eigenvalues) exist.

This is somewhat wonky as stated. Algebraic closure ensures that "an operator has eigenvalues", but so does any field where p_T has a root. Algebraic closure gives the stronger guarantee that T has dim V eigenvalues counted with multiplicity. I'd reframe to something like:

Q. Over an algebraically closed field (like C), what can we guarantee about the eigenvalues of any linear operator?
etc

But this was very stimulating—thank you! Our dataset doesn't include any deep math content.
