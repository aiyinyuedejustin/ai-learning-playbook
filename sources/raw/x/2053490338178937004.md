1/**Feynman Learning Method (refined prompt)**

“Explain **[insert topic]** in very simple terms, as if I’m a complete beginner. Then break it down with a quick example.

After that, ask me to explain it back in my own words. Identify any mistakes, missing points, or confusion in my explanation, and help me fix them step by step.”

### 2026-05-10T15:00:28.000Z · https://x.com/MRB_AI24/status/2053490361700585563

2/**First Principles Breakdown (refined prompt)**

“Act as an expert instructor. Explain **[insert topic]** using first principles thinking. Break it down into the most basic, fundamental truths that cannot be simplified further.

Then rebuild the topic step by step from those foundations so I understand *why it works*, not just what it is.

Avoid memorization, analogies without explanation, or surface-level summaries—focus on core logic and reasoning at each step.”

### 2026-05-10T15:00:31.000Z · https://x.com/MRB_AI24/status/2053490373453066327

3/**Active Recall Coach (refined prompt)**

“First, teach me **[insert topic]** in a clear and structured way.

Then switch into a quiz mode: ask me challenging questions that require active recall (not recognition or multiple choice unless necessary).

After I answer, evaluate my responses carefully. Point out what I got right, what I missed, and why. Then correct my misunderstandings and reinforce the key ideas with short explanations.

Repeat the cycle until my understanding is solid.”

### 2026-05-10T15:00:34.000Z · https://x.com/MRB_AI24/status/2053490385239019734

4/**Spaced Repetition Schedule (refined prompt)**

“Create a structured spaced repetition plan for learning **[insert topic]**.

Break it into a timeline that optimizes long-term memory retention:

* **Day 1–7 (initial learning phase):** what to study each day and how to actively engage with the material
* **Week 2–4 (reinforcement phase):** what to revisit, in what order, and how to test recall
* **Month 2+ (long-term retention phase):** what to periodically review and how often

Include:

* Specific review intervals (daily, every 2–3 days, weekly, monthly)
* Methods for reviewing (active recall, quizzes, teaching, problem-solving)
* A simple system to track progress and weak areas

Focus on efficiency—help me retain maximum knowledge with minimum wasted review time.”

5/**Smart Knowledge Summary (refined prompt)**

“Summarize **[insert topic]** into a structured, easy-to-understand format that prioritizes clarity and usability over detail.

Break it down into:

* Core concepts (the essential ideas I must know)
* Simple frameworks or structures that organize the topic logically
* Key mental models that help me understand and apply it in real situations
* Common mistakes or misconceptions to avoid
* A short ‘big picture’ summary that ties everything together

Keep it simple, memorable, and focused on practical understanding rather than excessive detail or jargon.”

6. Learning Acceleration Roadmap

“Create a structured learning roadmap to master [insert skill]. Break it into clear stages with defined goals, milestones, and checkpoints. For each stage, include exactly what to learn, in what order, and what to practice so I always know the next step without wasting time or guessing what comes next. Also include common mistakes to avoid and a simple way to measure progress at each level.”

7. Weakness & Gap Diagnostic

“Act as an evaluator for **[insert topic]**. Ask me a structured set of diagnostic questions that test both foundational understanding and applied thinking. Based on my answers, identify my weakest areas, misconceptions, and blind spots. Then break down exactly what I don’t understand and why it matters. Finally, give me targeted, high-impact steps, exercises, or concepts to fix each weakness in the most efficient way possible.”
